# Voice and marketing

## Trust first

Marketing comes from useful information: identify the item immediately, put
likely search terms near the front, state condition and decision-relevant facts,
reduce avoidable questions, and make delivery, payment, and contact clear. Use
one evidence-backed reason to consider the item when one exists; do not force a
selling line. Never promise a sale or present copy as the only reason an item
did not sell.

## Market register

- Honduras: use plain, neutral Spanish and a cordial Honduran shop register
  with `usted`: `escríbanos`, `tenemos`, and `con gusto le atendemos`. Avoid
  voseo, forced slang, and exaggerated promotion.
- United States: use natural plain US English. Keep it direct, useful, and
  low-pressure; avoid translated-sounding phrasing, hype, and stiff templates.

Keep lines short and vary their rhythm naturally in both markets.

## Natural description recipe

Write the product copy as four to seven short lines when the facts support
them. A strong description usually does this in order:

1. Names the product, condition, and strongest confirmed differentiator.
2. Gives one concrete, evidence-backed reason to consider it.
3. Adds decision details such as model, size, included items, color, price,
   pickup, delivery, shipping, or payment when known.
4. Ends with a low-pressure availability invitation.

Do not force a line when evidence is missing. Customer-facing copy must not use
audit language such as `no hay evidencia`, `principal ventaja`, or `posible
bloqueador`.

Natural CTA examples:

- Honduras: `Escríbanos para confirmar qué colores están disponibles.`
- United States: `Send a message to confirm it is still available.`

## Machine-language patterns

Rewrite these named patterns when they appear:

- Stacked adjective pairs: “fresca y cómoda”, “limpio y discreto”, “elegante y
  versátil”, “práctico y funcional”.
- Use-case menus: “para el trabajo, clases o una salida casual” and “ideal para
  cualquier ocasión”.
- Two-branch formulas: “ya sea para X o para Y”, “tanto para X como para Y”,
  and “no solo X sino también Y”.
- Copywriter verbs: “cuenta con”, “brinda”, “ofrece”, “destaca por”, and “lo
  convierte en”.
- Benefit endings on every line, unsupported appraisals, fake urgency, and
  metronomic noun-plus-description rhythm.

Use grounded alternatives: name what is visible, state what the user
confirmed, and omit the rest. `Vestido azul, nuevo, tallas M y L` is better
than an invented quality claim. `La etiqueta muestra talla M` is supported;
`tela cómoda` is unknown without evidence.

## Paired examples

### Honduras example

Input facts: a photo shows a blue dress; the user confirms it is new, available
in M and L, costs L650, and can be delivered in Tegucigalpa.

**Descripción mejorada**

Vestido azul nuevo.
Disponible en tallas M y L.
Precio: L650.
Entrega en Tegucigalpa según coordinación.
Escríbanos para confirmar qué colores están disponibles.

### United States example

Input facts: the photos show a black desk lamp; the user confirms it works,
includes the power cable, costs $25, and is available for Austin pickup.

**Improved description**

Black desk lamp in working condition.
Adjustable arm and weighted base are shown in the photos.
Includes the power cable.
$25. Austin pickup.
Send a message to confirm it is still available.

These examples do not add material, fit, comfort, audience, compatibility, or
use cases that were not confirmed or visible.
